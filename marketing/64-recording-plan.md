# 64 — Recording plan: your chess set, your iPhone, 30 minutes

These recordings replace 64's piece sounds with a real set. You record; I cut, clean,
level and build the sounds from them (soft and firm variants, slight differences every
time, a different character per board).

## What you need
- **A real wooden chess set**, ideally weighted pieces with felt bottoms, and a **wooden
  board**. If you can, also a **glass or stone surface** (marble table, stone windowsill)
  for the Marble board.
- **Your iPhone** with the **Voice Memos** app.
- **A quiet room**: no fridge hum, no traffic, no fan. Close the windows. Evenings are best.
- **A towel or blanket** under the board (kills table rumble).
- Optional: a **chess clock**, a **wooden box** for the pieces.

## Set up the iPhone (once)
1. Settings ▸ Apps ▸ Voice Memos ▸ Audio Quality: **Lossless**.
2. Settings ▸ Apps ▸ Voice Memos ▸ **Stereo Recording: on**. (If it isn't there, that's fine.)
3. Put the iPhone **flat on the table, about 20–30 cm from the board**, microphone (bottom
   edge) pointing at the board. Don't hold it: hands make noise.
4. Do a 5-second test: tap a piece once, listen back with headphones. If it's distorted,
   move the phone a bit further away.

## How to record
- **One Voice Memo per section below.** Name each one exactly as written (e.g. "02 pawn").
- **Say the section name out loud first**, then wait 2 seconds of silence.
- **Leave about 1 second of silence between every sound.** I cut them apart.
- **Don't talk, breathe away from the phone, keep your hands still between takes.**
- Do **every sound 8 times**, varying slightly where it says so.

## Send them to me
1. In Voice Memos, select each recording ▸ Share ▸ **Save to Files** (one folder, e.g. "64 sounds").
2. Upload the folder (or the files) here in the chat.
3. That's it. I'll send you back a before/after so you can hear the difference, then put
   them into the app.

## Tips for the best sound
- Felt-bottomed pieces on a wooden board give the classic soft "tock". Without felt you
  get a sharper click; both are fine, just tell me which set you used.
- If a take has a noise in it (cough, car, chair), just do one more; I'll pick the clean ones.
- Quieter is better than louder: I can raise the level, but I can't remove distortion.
