# 64 launch plan: Instagram Reels and TikTok

## 1. The idea in one line

**"Chess, across the table."** Every post shows real people playing face to face over an
iPad, then the app itself. The angle is human: put the phone down, play the person
in front of you. That is the thing chess.com and Lichess are not.

Be honest in the copy: Lichess and chess.com both have a pass-and-play mode, so don't
claim "the only app". Claim what is true and visible: a board made for the table,
pieces that feel real, a clock, a computer opponent, puzzles and game review.

short faceless product shots like `64-reel-seedance-hero.mp4`. Faces and hands are what
make AI video look fake. The plan below still works with an image post or a screen
recording in place of each AI clip.

1. **0–3 s, hook:** the AI clip (people playing) with one line of text on screen.

## 4. Prompts to make more content

### Claude or ChatGPT: hooks and captions

```
You are a short-form video strategist for a premium iPad chess app called 64. Its
promise: two people play face to face across one iPad, with a board that fills the
screen, real-feeling 3D pieces, wood sounds, a chess clock, a computer opponent,
checkmate puzzles and game review. Audience: casual chess players 18–40, couples,
families, friends at cafés and pubs. Write 20 on-screen hooks of at most 9 words
that make someone stop scrolling in the first second. Mix: POV jokes, relatable
moments, "date night idea", nostalgia, challenge ("tag a friend"). No hype words
like "revolutionary" or "game-changer". Then write a 1-line caption for each.
```

```
Here are my last 10 Reels with their views and average watch time: [paste].
Tell me which hooks worked and why, and write 10 new hooks in the style of the top 3.
```

### Higgsfield: new scenes (image first, then animate)

Image (GPT Image 2.5, medium, 9:16, about 0.5 credits). Attach the 64 board picture
as a reference so the iPad shows the real board:

```
Cinematic vertical photo, [SCENE AND LIGHT]. [WHO] sit across from each other at
[TABLE]. Between them an iPad lies flat on the table, its screen showing exactly
the brown-and-cream chessboard with ivory and ebony pieces from the reference image.
[ACTION AND EMOTION]. Shot on 35mm, shallow depth of field, natural skin, no text.
```

Scene ideas that fit the brand: rooftop at sunset; camping table with a lantern;
grandmother and granddaughter on a porch; chess club in a library; beach café;
airport gate; two old friends reunited; a classroom chess club; snowy cabin by the fire.

Images look most real when prompted as phones see the world: "Candid iPhone photo …,
natural light, slight grain, unposed, no faces". Top-down table shots work best.

Video: Seedance 2.0 (Fast, 720p, 5 s, 9:16, no sound, 12.5 credits; standard quality is
22.5), using a faceless image as the start frame:

```
Real product film. Slow, steady dolly-in toward [THE SCENE]. A soft band of warm light
drifts across the table. Physically accurate reflections, no morphing, the screen image
stays perfectly still and sharp. Shot on a cinema camera, 24fps, shallow depth of field.
```

Avoid faces and hand gestures in AI video; keep it to camera moves and light.

## 6. Will it make passive income?

Honest answer: it can make money, but it won't be passive at the start. Early on, the
work is posting consistently, reading the numbers and improving the hooks.

How the money works (App Store, Small Business Program: Apple keeps 15%):

- A typical free app with a trial converts 2–5% of downloads into paying subscribers.
- Mixed monthly and yearly plans average roughly $1.50–2.00 per subscriber per month after Apple's cut.

| Downloads per month | Paying subscribers added (≈3%) | Revenue after 6 months* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | 30 | ≈ $250–350 / month |
| 10,000 | 300 | ≈ $2,500–3,500 / month |
| 50,000 | 1,500 | ≈ $12,000–17,000 / month |

\*Subscribers accumulate month over month, minus cancellations (assumed ~10% a month).
These are planning numbers, not a promise; most apps never get past the first row.

What helps 64:

- It photographs and films beautifully, and it's a different angle from every other
  chess app ("look up from your phone").
- Chess is big and steady: hundreds of millions of players, and huge chess TikTok/Instagram communities.

What holds it back:

- iPad only: a smaller market than iPhone.
- Strong free competitors for playing online.

Good next steps after launch:

- An iPhone version of the puzzles and review.
- Localization (German, Spanish, French).
- Asking chess creators for honest reviews: send them a free year of Pro.
